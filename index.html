Privacy

This app doesn’t collect anything about you or your child. There are no accounts, names, email addresses, ads, cookies or analytics.

What it saves, and where. Words read, aliens collected, lesson settings and today’s session are saved in this browser, on this device only.

Backup and transfer links. These links hold that same progress (word counts, alien names, the lesson). They go only where you send them. The progress is in the part of the link after “#”, which browsers don’t send to the website.

Hear it. The ear button uses your device’s built-in voice. On some devices and browsers, those voices are provided online by the device or browser maker.

The website. Like any website, the host may keep standard visit logs (such as IP addresses).

Deleting. Remove the app from the home screen, or clear this site’s data in your browser settings. That deletes everything it saved.

    How to help your reader learn with Blendaroo

    Ask the reader to sound out each sound as they slide their finger. Encourage them to keep their voice going without stopping: “mmmaaap.” After sounding out the word and hearing what they said, they should say it again fast.

    The words include three types: “decodable,” “made-up” (but following typical decodable rules), and “heart words.”

    A heart word has a part that doesn’t follow the rules taught so far. That part gets a heart and a pink dot or line: it’s learned “by heart,” and the rest is sounded out as usual. Many heart words come in families (could, would, should). This is a common approach in structured-literacy teaching. To practice them, choose the “Heart words” lessons.

    Many curricula also refer to “sight,” “sticky,” or “trick” words. These are typically a mix of common heart words and other common words that are “decodable” (spelled phonetically).

    To focus on common words, choose “Common words” and select the word list to draw from (top 10 most common, top 60, top 200, etc.). Then you can select if you want to focus on common words that are “decodable,” “heart words,” or both.

    Custom lesson lists

    One lesson per row. The first row names the columns: name, pattern, decodable, made-up and heart words. Only decodable is needed. Words can be split by commas or spaces. An optional spoken column says how the 👂 button should say each made-up word, in the same order (like suld for sulled). Leave made-up empty and Blendaroo picks made-up words from the By phoneme lessons with the same pattern. A lesson with fewer than 30 decodable words is topped up with real words from those lessons. Heart words get their hearts from Blendaroo’s own lists. For Excel or Numbers, export as CSV first. A Google Sheet must be shared as “Anyone with the link”. Lists stay on this device.
